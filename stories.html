The house on the high ground

Remembered by the elders of the Casa do Alto · Family tradition

They always called it the Casa do Alto — the house on the high ground — because from its door you could see the whole valley fall away toward the town. The story passed down is that the family took the high fields when no one else wanted them, worked them harder than anyone, and never let the name go, even after some of them came down to the town for the mills.

Whether the house came before the name or the name before the house, no one now living can say. But every branch of the family seems to carry some version of it — which is its own kind of evidence that it mattered.

A gentle guide

What makes a story worth keeping

Almost anything, honestly. But if you're not sure where to start, these tend to be treasures:

  • Oral histories — what an elder told you about their parents and grandparents.
  • Everyday detail — trades, nicknames, recipes, songs, the way someone spoke.
  • Turning points — a marriage, an emigration, a move to the town, a hardship survived.
  • Objects & documents — a photograph, a letter, an entry in a family bible.
  • Sayings and traditions — the small inherited things that vanish if unrecorded.

Don't worry about polish or certainty. Tell us what you remember and how you know it — whether it's a proven fact or "the way my grandmother always told it." We'll label it honestly and keep it safe.
