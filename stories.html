From Freixo da Serra to America

The family's journey across the Atlantic · Research in progress

Sometime in the last century, one of our own climbed down from the granite highlands, boarded a ship, and crossed the ocean to the United States — joining the great wave of Portuguese who built new lives in the mill towns of New England and the cities of New Jersey. They carried a language, a faith, and a village name: Freixo da Serra.

This is the family's central story, and the one we are working hardest to document — from the passenger manifest that first named the village, to the census pages and naturalization papers that trace the family's American beginnings.

Names, dates, ships, and places are still being confirmed — add what your side of the family remembers.

A one-way passage to Brazil

The Joaquim Guerra line · Unverified

Not every departure led to America. The family always said Joaquim sailed for São Paulo around 1901 and was never seen again in the Serra. We keep his story here as part of the record, and we are still searching for his passenger manifest — and, we hope, for his descendants in Brazil.

A gentle guide

What makes a story worth keeping

Almost anything, honestly. But if you're not sure where to start, these tend to be treasures:

  • Oral histories — what an elder told you about their parents and grandparents.
  • Everyday detail — trades, nicknames, recipes, songs, the way someone spoke.
  • Turning points — a marriage, an emigration, a move to the town, a hardship survived.
  • Objects & documents — a photograph, a letter, an entry in a family bible.
  • Sayings and traditions — the small inherited things that vanish if unrecorded.

Don't worry about polish or certainty. Tell us what you remember and how you know it — whether it's a proven fact or "the way my grandmother always told it." We'll label it honestly and keep it safe.
