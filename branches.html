The lines of the family

A large family is really a set of branches — lines that share a common root but grow their own way. Here are the branches we are tracing, the key ancestors who anchor each one, and the paths that carried some of them far from the Serra da Estrela.

🇺🇸 The family's main — and best-documented — migration story

From Freixo da Serra to America — the Ludlow, Massachusetts Branch

The family's great journey across the Atlantic led to Ludlow, Hampden County, Massachusetts — a major destination for Portuguese immigrants, many from the Serra da Estrela, who came to work at the Ludlow Manufacturing Associates jute mills. This is now the most extensively documented emigration line from the family, and it is featured here more prominently than the still-unverified Brazil line.

Maternal & paternal lines

How we record both sides

A family history that follows only the surname misses half the story. Where the records allow, we trace maternal lines — the mothers, grandmothers, and the families they came from — alongside the paternal Guerra line. Married-in families such as the Nunes, Figueiredo, Lopes, and Ramos names appear in the tree for exactly this reason.

On the family tree, you can filter by branch to follow a single line, and each person's card links to their parents, spouse, and children so you can move between the branches freely.

Migration paths

From Freixo da Serra outward

From a single mountain parish, the family spread in stages — but the journey that shaped us most was the crossing to America. These are the paths we trace, with the United States as the family's principal emigration story.

  • Overseas — U.S. Serra da Estrela → Ellis Island / New York → New England textile cities & New Jersey Primary migration — the family's main path across the Atlantic.
  • Regional Freixo da Serra → Gouveia town → Lisbon (work in the wool and textile trades, then the capital).
  • Local Freixo da Serra → Figueiró da Serra & neighbouring parishes (marriage and land ties).

Other destinations

Not everyone who left went to America. One documented line went to Brazil, and it remains part of the family record.

  • Brazil Serra da Estrela → Porto → São Paulo Under review — the Joaquim Guerra line (emigrated c. 1901); still being traced, and its descendants actively sought.
